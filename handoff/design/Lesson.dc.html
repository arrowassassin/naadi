Every blood gas answers the same five questions, in the same order. Learn the order and the vignette solves itself.

1

Read the pH

Below 7.35 is acidemia; above 7.45 is alkalemia.

2

Find the primary process

Low HCO₃⁻ with acidemia is metabolic; high PCO₂ with acidemia is respiratory.

3

Calculate the anion gap

In a metabolic acidosis, anion gap = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻), normally about 8–12 mEq/L. A raised gap means an unmeasured acid has been added.

4

Check compensation

Winters' formula: expected PCO₂ = 1.5 × HCO₃⁻ + 8, ± 2. A measured PCO₂ outside that range means a second, respiratory disorder.

5

Compare the deltas

If the rise in anion gap doesn't match the fall in HCO₃⁻, a second metabolic process is hiding underneath.

Summary · written by Naadi from your 5 highlights

pH → primary process → anion gap → Winters → delta. Gap is Na minus Cl and bicarb; Winters is 1.5 × bicarb + 8.
