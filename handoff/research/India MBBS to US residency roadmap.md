# Bhubaneswar to Bay Area: Lock In 2028

**The fastest reliable route is a 20-month sprint. Start now.** Pass Step 1 in mid/late January 2027 and Step 2 CK around May 2027. Take OET Medicine on 19 December 2026 and lock in a 2027 ECFMG Pathway by 31 January 2027. Do 4–8 weeks of US observerships in May–August 2027, apply through ERAS in September 2027, and match in March 2028 into **categorical Internal Medicine** on a **J-1 visa**, with Family Medicine as a parallel safety. Residency would start July 2028. The odds are real but tight. In 2026, non-US IMGs who needed visa sponsorship matched at only **54.4%**, a five-year low ([NRMP 2026 press release](https://www.nrmp.org/about/news/2026/03/nrmp-releases-results-of-the-2026-main-residency-match-for-more-than-38000-future-residents/)). Her profile starts below that average: no US exposure, no research, and a mid-tier school. Step 2 CK, US letters and how broadly she applies will decide her outcome. Her MBBS percentage and NEET PG rank will not. Residency is not the end of the visa problem. A J-1 brings a two-year home-return rule that India will not waive ([CGI San Francisco](https://www.cgisf.gov.in/page/statement-of-need-certificates-for-medical-doctors/)). Most J-1 doctors therefore take a 3-year waiver job in an underserved area. The India green-card queue then adds a decade or more ([F1Jobs, Nov 2026 bulletin](https://www.f1jobs.io/resources/blog/november-2026-visa-bulletin-explained-eb2-eb3-india)). Realistic Bay Area practice is therefore a mid-2030s goal. The ChatGPT roadmap got most rules right. It was too relaxed about OET, Pathway and visa timing, it listed only "reach" programs in California, and it left out the green-card backlog.

## Certification hinges on Pathway timing, not just exams

ECFMG certification has four parts. The first is an accepted application with diploma and transcript verified directly with SOA through MyIntealth. The others are a Step 1 pass, a Step 2 CK pass, and an accepted, unexpired Pathway that includes OET Medicine ([ECFMG requirements](https://ecfmg.org/2026ib/certification-requirements.html)). Since January 2026, IMGs register for USMLE through **FSMB**, not ECFMG ([ECFMG News](https://www.ecfmg.org/news/category/pathways/)). One finding changes the plan from the old roadmap. **No USMLE pass is needed to apply for a 2027 Pathway.** The passes are needed only by the Rank Order List deadline of the Match she enters ([2027 FAQ](https://apps.wdoms.org/certification-pathways/faqs.html)). ECFMG has **not announced 2028 Pathways**. It publishes Pathways one year at a time and tells future applicants to "monitor the ECFMG website" ([ECFMG news](https://www.ecfmg.org/news/category/pathways/)). How long a 2027 Pathway stays valid is also not settled. A secondary source reports it expires **31 Dec 2029** ([The Match Guy](https://thematchguy.com/pathways-ecfmg-certification/)). ECFMG's official table stops at the 2026 season, which expires 31 Dec 2028 ([ECFMG expiration](https://corp.ecfmg.org/certification-pathways/expiration.html)). Either date covers a March 2028 rank deadline. She should email ECFMG to confirm the exact expiry, and to confirm that a 2027 Pathway stays valid if she skips the 2027 Match.

**Which Pathway?** **Pathway 1** fits if she holds permanent, unsupervised registration with the NMC or a state council that was valid on or after 1 Jan 2022 ([Pathway 1](https://corp.ecfmg.org/certification-pathways/pathway-1.html)). ECFMG has not stated whether Indian permanent registration counts, so she should ask. **Pathway 3** fits if she graduated on or after 1 Jan 2024 and SOA appears on ECFMG's "Medical Schools Eligible for 2027 Pathways" list. It needs a school attestation by 15 Feb 2027 ([Pathways 3–5](https://certification.ecfmg.org/certification-pathways/pathways-3-5.html)). **Pathway 6** is the fallback. It needs six real, in-person Mini-CEX encounters, each assessed by a licensed physician ([2027 FAQ](https://apps.wdoms.org/certification-pathways/faqs.html)).

**Should she take OET in December? Yes, as insurance.** Fees for OET and the Pathway total about $1,325. Those fees buy certainty under known rules, and the OET score would probably also count for a 2028 Pathway if one is offered. This last point is inferred from the 2027 window, which accepts tests taken on or after 1 Jan 2025 ([2027 FAQ](https://apps.wdoms.org/certification-pathways/faqs.html)). There is one real cost. OET falls on about day 34 of the Step 1 sprint. OET@Home is not an option, because India has test venues ([2027 FAQ](https://apps.wdoms.org/certification-pathways/faqs.html)). The 5 Dec sitting has already closed. If she cannot book 19 Dec, a computer-based December slot is the fallback, with booking 7 days ahead and results in about 48 hours. That fallback rests on a secondary source ([TalkDrill](https://www.talkdrill.com/blog/exams/oet/exam-dates-2026/)).

The attempt rules make a first-attempt pass essential. A candidate gets at most 3 attempts at a Step within 12 months, and 4 failed attempts at any Step end USMLE eligibility. A passed Step cannot be retaken for a higher score. All exams must be completed within 7 years of the first pass ([USMLE eligibility](https://www.usmle.org/bulletin-information/eligibility); [ECFMG time limit](https://ecfmg.org/2026ib/time-limit-complete-exams.html)). Many programs screen for first-attempt passes, so a single failure shrinks her program list far more than a two-week delay would.

**Step 3 early: only for an H-1B strategy.** An H-1B for residency generally needs Step 3 passed before the petition is filed ([Visas notes, FSMB/attorneys](https://www.fsmb.org/siteassets/baw-speaker-presentation/session-8-imgs-fsmb-attorneys-nov-2025.pdf)). For her, Step 3 would mean being certified by about July 2027 and then testing in the US during an observership trip. The upside is access to the small set of H-1B programs and no two-year home-return rule later. The downsides are about $955 plus extra US living costs. It also takes study time from Step 2 CK and letter-building, and any failure is visible to programs. The H-1B route itself is fragile (see the visa section). Recommendation: attempt Step 3 before ERAS only if her Step 2 CK is about 250 or higher, she is certified by mid-2027, and she has named H-1B programs she would actually rank. Otherwise she should take Step 3 during PGY-1.

## Step 1 in 60 days, then a 12-week Step 2 CK build

The roadmap's 4–6 months for Step 1 is too slow for a 2028 Match. A NEET PG candidate already has a year of recent, high-volume recall. The real task is to retrain that knowledge for USMLE-style questions that test mechanisms and two-step reasoning. The [60-day study-plan spreadsheet](https://claude.ai/artifact/GMbf87WTgdpoHVLLV29zNG) sets the daily load. The exam date should depend on readiness checks, not the calendar. A CBSSA of about 62–68 is described as the "low passing range", and about 70 is a safer bar. These figures are informal and student-reported ([Elite Medical Prep](https://elitemedicalprep.com/nbme-25-nbme-30-best-predictor-for-passing-step-1/)). She should sit the exam only after two fresh forms land there, with the Free 120 taken 3–7 days before the exam.

**Step 2 CK phase plan (about 12 weeks, Feb–May 2027):**

|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Baseline CCSSA. UWorld CK by system. Learn US management conventions: screening, consent, ethics, safety. | Baseline score |
| 3–8 | 80–120 UWorld CK questions a day, timed and random. Divine Intervention podcasts and Mehlman CK PDFs for weak areas. Anki for misses. | A CCSSA every ~2 weeks |

## US exposure means observerships, letters and a visa booked now

MBBS graduates can generally do only **observerships**, which are shadowing with no patient contact. A B-1 visa covers unpaid hands-on clerkships only for *students* ([AMA](https://ama-assn.org/life-career/observership-program-listings-international-medical-graduates); [Temple ISSS citing 9 FAM](https://global.temple.edu/isss/faculty-staff-and-researchers/temple-links/medical-activities)). The value of an observership lies mostly in the **letter** it produces. Specialty-specific letters are a top interview-selection factor, cited by 84–86% of program directors ([NRMP 2024 PD Survey](https://www.nrmp.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/2024-PD-Survey-Report-narrative_Final.pdf); [2026 PD Survey](https://www.nrmp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026-PD-Survey-Report-Narrative-FINAL.pdf)). UCSF-Fresno IM requires at least 4 weeks of observership in at least 2 settings from IMGs without US rotations ([UCSF Fresno](https://fresno.ucsf.edu/internal-medicine/applicants)). She should choose 4-week blocks with one attending, and ask about the letter policy in writing before paying.

**Recommendation.** Apply to **100–150 categorical IM programs** and about **30 FM programs**. Add one more field only if her observerships show real interest in Peds, Psych, Neuro or Path, because those need specialty-specific letters. The main reason is the data. Matched and unmatched IMGs differ by only **5–10 points** on Step 2 CK. Getting interviews is the bottleneck, and IM has the most interviews to win. ERAS charges $11 per program for the first 30 and $30 each after that, so 100 IM programs cost $2,430 ([Lecturio](https://www.lecturio.com/blog/eras-2027-timeline-residency-application-strategy/)). IM gets 3 gold and 12 silver signals ([AAMC signaling](https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-residencies-eras/program-signaling-2027-myeras-application-season)). She should spend gold signals on realistic IMG-friendly programs, not on UCSF or Stanford.

**California targets.** Bay Area academic and Kaiser programs take almost no non-US IMGs. For example, Kaiser NorCal Oakland and San Francisco IM show about 0% IMGs, and Santa Clara Valley about 3%. The realistic California list is in the Central Valley and Inland Empire, and almost all of it is J-1 only: **San Joaquin General** (Stockton area, about 1.5 hours from the Bay), Kern Medical, St Agnes and UCSF-Fresno, Adventist Tulare, the Prime West sites, and UCR/St Bernardine. **Highland** in Oakland is a stretch, with 577 interviews for 30 seats ([ResidencyMatch.ai CA list](https://www.residencymatch.ai/img-friendly/by-state/ca); [FREIDA Highland](https://freida.ama-assn.org/program/1400531051); [Kern](https://www.kernmedical.com/academics/residency-fellowship-programs/internal-medicine/international-residents/)). Most of her interviews will come from IMG-heavy states such as NY, NJ, MI, OH, PA, IL and TX. She should verify each program's J-1 and graduation-year rules in FREIDA. Training outside California does not stop her from getting a California license later.

## After the Match, visas set the Bay Area timeline

**J-1 is the default.** It needs ECFMG/Intealth sponsorship and a Statement of Need from MoHFW in New Delhi, which must name the **exact specialty**. It is capped at about 7 years and carries the **INA 212(e) two-year home-residency rule**. MoHFW "will not issue" a no-objection letter (NORI) "under any circumstance" ([ECFMG EVSP](https://ecfmg.org/evsp/applying-general.html); [CGI San Francisco](https://www.cgisf.gov.in/page/statement-of-need-certificates-for-medical-doctors/)). To stay after a J-1, she needs a waiver job or two years back in India. California's Conrad 30 program accepts applications 1–31 October each year, reviews them in order of receipt with rural priority, and requires practice in a shortage area (HPSA, MUA or MUP) ([HCAI](https://hcai.ca.gov/workforce-capacity/california-primary-care-office/j-1-visa-waiver-program/)). The federal HHS waiver slowed to a months-long backlog in 2025–26 ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-industry/hhs-exchange-visitor-program-visa-waiver-j1-h1b-delays-foreign-doctors-deadline/)).

**H-1B for residency is fragile.** The $100,000 fee proclamation applies to new petitions for doctors outside the US, which describes her if she matches from India. It does not apply to a J-1 to H-1B change of status after training ([FSMB/attorneys](https://www.fsmb.org/siteassets/baw-speaker-presentation/session-8-imgs-fsmb-attorneys-nov-2025.pdf)). The courts have struck it down repeatedly. It was vacated on 8 June 2026, and a stay was denied on 24 July ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/first-circuit-blocks-reinstatement-of-100k-h-1b-fee/)). It was renewed on 18 Sept 2026 to run until 21 Sept 2027 ([Klasko](https://www.klaskolaw.com/h-1b-100000-fee-extended-one-year/)). The renewal was vacated and enjoined on 30 Sept 2026 ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/stuartanderson/2026/10/01/judge-strikes-down-latest-version-of-100000-h-1b-immigration-fee/)). **As of November 2026 the fee is not being collected, but litigation continues.** A separate proposed rule for a $103,265 fee is also pending. AAMC's January 2026 report that hospitals were cutting back on H-1B hiring is now history, but programs ranking applicants for 2028 will stay cautious ([AAMC](https://aamc.org/news/hospitals-and-health-systems-depend-h-1b-visa-sponsored-physicians-so-what-happens-now)).

**Green card.** In the November 2026 bulletin, EB-2 India is at **1 Nov 2013**. EB-1 India is at 1 Feb 2023. More than 356,000 approved EB-2 India petitions are waiting ([F1Jobs, verify on travel.state.gov](https://www.f1jobs.io/resources/blog/november-2026-visa-bulletin-explained-eb2-eb3-india)). A physician national interest waiver (NIW) still leaves her waiting 10 or more years. Faster routes are EB-1 (for academic achievement) or a spouse who is a US citizen or green-card holder.

**California licensure.** IMG residents must get a Postgraduate Training License (PTL) within 180 days of starting. They become eligible for the full Physician and Surgeon license after **24 months** of approved training, and Step 3 is required ([MBC](https://www.mbc.ca.gov/Licensing/Physicians-and-Surgeons/Apply/); [UC Davis GME](https://health.ucdavis.edu/gme/camb_licensing.html)). AB 2386, a California alternative pathway for IMGs, died in Senate Appropriations on 13 Aug 2026 ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260ab2386)). The pathways passed in about 18 other states all require prior licensed practice or training abroad, so none help a fresh MBBS graduate ([FSMB chart](https://inte.fsmb.org/siteassets/advocacy/policies/states-with-enacted-and-proposed-additional-img-licensure-pathways-key-issue-chart.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The old roadmap treated this as a staged journey, done one step after another. The 2026 rules make it a parallel race with a few deadlines that cannot be undone. Pathway eligibility, OET booking and B-visa queues are all decided in the next two weeks. Each is cheap to secure now and costly to miss. So the most useful thing she can do this month is administrative, not academic. After that, one variable outweighs the rest: a strong Step 2 CK on the first attempt, turned into interviews through broad IM and FM applications and US letters.

The Bay Area goal should not shape her residency list. The J-1 waiver and the India green-card queue are the real limits on where she can live. In practice, the first attending job will go wherever a shortage-area employer sponsors her, often in the Central Valley. A Bay Area career is realistic as the second job, not the first. If the family plans with a 10-year horizon, the effort is worthwhile. If they need certainty in 3 years, this route cannot give it.
